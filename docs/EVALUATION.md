# TCF Canada writing evaluation guide

Last source check: **2026-10-17**.

This document separates official public information from this template's pedagogical method.

## Official public format

TCF Canada Expression écrite lasts 60 minutes and contains three tasks:

| Task | Expected production | Published word range |
|---|---|---:|
| Tâche 1 | A message that describes, recounts, and/or explains for one or more identified recipients | 60–120 |
| Tâche 2 | An article, letter, note, or similar text recounting an experience or event, with comments, opinions, or arguments serving a purpose | 120–150 |
| Tâche 3 | A text comparing two viewpoints from two documents about a social issue and giving the candidate's position | 120–180 |

France Éducation international states that candidates are expected to communicate clearly, provide
requested information, describe/recount/explain, justify choices or positions, connect ideas
coherently, express and argue an opinion, and use vocabulary and structures appropriate to the task.

## Official correction process and limits on AI estimates

France Éducation international describes two independent, double-blind evaluations of written
expression. Each evaluator assigns each of the three tasks a level from `A1 not achieved` to `C2`.
The six task judgments are combined into a final score and level; a sufficiently large disagreement
can trigger a third correction. The detailed combination formula and numerical domain weights are
not published.

Consequences for this template:

- do not invent points such as “grammar 5/5” and present them as official;
- do not invent task weights;
- do not derive a final `/20` score from one isolated task;
- do not derive an NCLC level from one isolated task;
- even a complete mock-exam projection must remain broad, pedagogical, and non-official;
- store an official score only when it comes from an actual official result supplied by the learner.

- [France Éducation international — candidate manual, correction section](https://www.france-education-international.fr/document/manuelcandidattoutes20dc3a9clinaisonsi)

## Evidence domains

The correction should organize evidence into three domains used in TCF expression assessment:

### Linguistic

- range and precision of vocabulary;
- grammatical control;
- spelling and punctuation;
- sentence construction and elaboration.

### Pragmatic

- task fulfilment and communicative outcome;
- coherence and cohesion;
- progression and thematic development;
- explanation, justification, comparison, and argumentation where required.

### Sociolinguistic

- fit with the communication situation;
- relationship with the recipient;
- genre conventions;
- tone and register.

These domains are evidence headings, not a secret points grid.

## Pedagogical two-pass method

### Pass 1 — communication

Read without line editing. Record:

- completion of requested actions;
- clarity of the result;
- development and progression;
- coherence and cohesion;
- genre, recipient, tone, and register;
- source use and comparison for Tâche 3.

### Pass 2 — language control

Record:

- vocabulary range, precision, and collocation;
- grammatical accuracy and sentence variety;
- spelling and punctuation;
- frequency, recurrence, and communicative impact of errors.

Then give a holistic range such as `B1–B2`, explain the limiting evidence, and choose no more than
five priorities. Do not average checklist entries.

## Preliminary admissibility-risk check

Flag a **risk**, rather than declaring an official outcome, when relevant:

- a task is missing;
- the response is off topic;
- required actions are absent;
- the word range is not respected;
- handwriting is illegible, when applicable;
- wording from the prompt or sources is copied rather than reformulated;
- a memorized or plagiarized text appears to replace the requested response.

The official sample defines a word as a set of signs between two spaces. Use that rule when a simple
mechanical count is needed.

## Task-specific checks

### Tâche 1

- Is the reason for the message immediately clear?
- Are all requested details/actions present?
- Does the opening, tone, and closing fit the recipient?
- Does the message describe, recount, and/or explain as requested?

### Tâche 2

- Is there an identifiable event or experience with progression?
- Do comments, opinions, or arguments serve the stated purpose?
- Are genre, readership, title (when appropriate), tone, and ending coherent?
- Are there enough concrete details without leaving the prompt?

### Tâche 3

Require the prompt and both documents. Check separately:

- faithful coverage of document 1;
- faithful coverage of document 2;
- an explicit relationship between the viewpoints;
- genuine reformulation rather than copied fragments;
- a clear personal position;
- developed support through explanation, consequence, or example;
- logical progression and a compatible conclusion.

Without the two documents, language can be corrected, but a complete holistic range should be
withheld because source fidelity and comparison cannot be verified.

## Required wording in feedback

Use the configured feedback language to communicate these statements:

- `Pedagogical level range for this task: ...`
- `This assessment is not official.`
- `One isolated task cannot determine the final /20 score or NCLC level.`

When relevant, add `Admissibility risk: ...` and explain the evidence.
